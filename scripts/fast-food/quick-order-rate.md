# quick-order-rate: Order and rate

Kinds: fast-food. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 5 steps, 23 s. New.

## Idea
The try-and-rate format: the most ordered item held up, a hook with the promise of an honest score, the
cross-section, the menu board with prices, and a score out of ten with the price and where.

## Audience moment
Someone hungry, choosing between two nearby counters, wanting the honest answer in under 30 s. The format of the
most-followed small-venue reviewer (fast-food.md [F17] [F18]); its value is that the score is honest.

## Why these steps in this order
1. `hold`: the item on screen first; size is clear in a hand.
2. `hook`: "rating it honestly, out of ten" is the promise the viewer waits for.
3. `inside`: the cross-section, the proof of the filling.
4. `menu`: the prices, so the score can be weighed against the cost.
5. `score`: the payoff, score plus price plus where.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `hold`: hero-push. Low, medium to close; cut to the selfie.
- `hook`: eye-level selfie, static; cut to the back camera.
- `inside`: cut-reveal. High, close to detail push-in, ends in a whip.
- `menu`: rail-truck along the board, eye-level, medium, truck-right; lands out of the whip; cut.
- `score`: high selfie, static.
Deliberate transitions: whip (1).

## Notes
- The lines never fix a score; the creator gives their own score out of ten, and a middling one is fine
  (fast-food.md: a score must match the reaction).
- No bite step: the bite is implied between `inside` and `score`. A bite step would push the length to 28 s,
  over the 20 to 25 s that suits this kind (fast-food.md: under 30 s performs best [F10]).

## Alternatives considered
- Menu board before the bite: the research order puts the bite first, since the price means more once the
  viewer has seen what it buys.
- A spoken score per item: that is `quick-budget-run`.
