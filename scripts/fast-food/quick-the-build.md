# quick-the-build: The build

Kinds: fast-food. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 6 steps, 28 s. New (behind the counter).

## Idea
Watch the signature item made from start to finish: the grill or fryer, the cook stacking it, the wrap, the
cross-section, the bite, and how to order it.

## Audience moment
Viewers who like process videos and want proof the food is made fresh. Prep from start to final product is a
named food truck and counter format (fast-food.md [F26]); "the way the cooks make it" doubles as the order tip.

## Why these steps in this order
1. `grill`: sizzle and sound, the hook.
2. `build`: the layers going on, the story.
3. `wrap`: the hand-over moment.
4. `inside`: the layers we watched, now as a cross-section.
5. `bite`: the reaction.
6. `outro`: name, price and how to order it.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `grill`: steam-hold variant. High, close, static; cut behind the cook.
- `build`: counter-over-shoulder. Over-shoulder, medium to close push-in; cut overhead.
- `wrap`: hands-at-work. Top-down, close, static; match-cut on the same order opened.
- `inside`: cut-reveal. High, close to detail push-in; push-through.
- `bite`: eye-level selfie that opens with the food filling the lens and pulls out to the face.
- `outro`: high selfie, static. Two spoken steps in a row, allowed.
Deliberate transitions: match-cut, push-through (2).

## Consent
`grill` and `build` film staff at work; both directions say to ask first. Staff are shown as hands and from
behind, never as a face close-up.

## Alternatives considered
- A sped-up build (fast-food.md mentions time-lapse): the kit has no speed field per step; the AI edit can speed
  up `build` if it wants.
- A spoken cook line: depends on staff willing to talk; captions carry the layers instead.
- "Speed run" (order to food in hand, timed): close to `quick-truck-find`'s hand-off; left out to keep the five
  scripts different in purpose.
