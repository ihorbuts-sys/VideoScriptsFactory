# quick-budget-run: Ten dollar run

Kinds: fast-food. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 6 steps, 28 s. New (the combo or price check).

## Idea
A set budget, how much food it buys: the budget said up front, the menu board, two items scored in pip, the
receipt as proof, and the one item to get.

## Audience moment
Students and anyone counting dollars: "how far does ten dollars go here". Price on camera matters more for this
kind than any other (fast-food.md [F14] [F16]); the receipt makes the claim checkable.

## Why these steps in this order
1. `hook`: the budget is the hook; it has to be said in the first 3 s.
2. `board`: the prices, so the viewer plays along.
3. `first`: item one with a score.
4. `second`: the main item with a score.
5. `receipt`: the total, proof.
6. `best`: the verdict: the one to order.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `hook`: eye-level selfie, static; cut to the back camera.
- `board`: rail-truck below the board, low, medium, truck-left; cut (low to top-down is three rows).
- `first`: top-down, close, static, spoken in pip; ends in a whip.
- `second`: hero-push out of the whip. Low, medium to close, spoken in pip; cut.
- `receipt`: top-down, detail, static; cut to the selfie.
- `best`: high selfie, static.
Deliberate transitions: whip (1).

## Checker note
The first draft had `board` at eye level, the same angle and size as the selfie before it; the checker counts
that as the same pose even across the camera flip, so the board moved to a low angle, which is also where a menu
board above a counter is.

## Alternatives considered
- Five dollars: too few items at most counters sampled; ten is the line from fast-food.md.
- Three items: three pip steps leave no room for the receipt inside 30 s.
- Scores as captions only: the score is the format; it has to be spoken.
