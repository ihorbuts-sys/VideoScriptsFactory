# quick-truck-find: Find the truck

Kinds: fast-food (written for `food-truck`). `featuresMenuItem: false`. 6 steps, 28 s. New.

## Idea
Where the truck is today and whether the line is worth it: the walk-up, ordering at the window, the hand-off,
unwrapping on the curb or in the car, the bite, and where it parks with its hours.

## Audience moment
Lunch-hour viewers near the truck's spot, or someone who heard about the truck and cannot find it. A truck moves;
the location and hours are the facts they act on (fast-food.md: showing where the truck parks is a named format
[F26]).

## Why these steps in this order
1. `truck`: the truck itself, the theme in the first frame.
2. `window`: the order, with the menu visible.
3. `handoff`: the order coming down out of the window, the satisfying beat of a truck.
4. `unwrap`: what came in the box.
5. `bite`: the honest reaction.
6. `where`: where it parks and the hours, with the truck behind.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `truck`: walk-up push-in. Eye-level, wide to medium; cut to the POV at the window.
- `window`: POV, medium, static, spoken in pip while ordering; cut low.
- `handoff`: low, close, static under the ledge; match-cut on the tray.
- `unwrap`: the same tray from straight above, top-down close to detail push-in; cut to the selfie.
- `bite`: eye-level selfie, medium to close push-in.
- `where`: high selfie with the truck behind, static.
Deliberate transitions: match-cut (1).

## Faces and consent
`truck` tells the creator to keep faces in the line out of frame; a line reads from backs and the menu.
Truck staff appear only as hands in `handoff`.

## Alternatives considered
- Opening on the food (hook rule): for a truck the place is the news, and the truck is a strong first frame.
- A whip from the truck to the window: the walk-up already moves; a whip on top reads as a trick.
- The window-to-car order from fast-food.md has five steps; the hand-off is split from the unwrap because the two
  angles (low under the ledge, top-down in the lap) make a clean match-cut.
