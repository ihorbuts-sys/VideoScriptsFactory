# shop-find-of-the-day: Find of the day

Kinds: shopping. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 6 steps, 24 s.

## Idea
The thrift and vintage format: one piece, the proof (label, condition) and the price. The find is the
story, the shop is where to get the next one.

## Audience moment
Someone who likes second-hand shopping and follows finds. They decide whether the shop is worth a
dig: real labels, real prices, a real rail [N27][N28].

## Why this order
1. `find`: the find held up is the hook [N27].
2. `rail`: the rail shows it is a real find, not staged [N28].
3. `label`: the label or maker's mark is the proof.
4. `tag`: the price, the reason a find is a find.
5. `why`: the creator's reason in one line.
6. `shopfront`: the shop and how to get there.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `find` (pov-pick): pov, medium to close, push-in, 4 s, out by cut.
- `rail` (rail-truck): eye-level, medium to medium, truck-right, 4 s, out by cut.
- `label` (whip-between-details): high, detail to detail, static, 3 s, out by whip.
- `tag` (whip-between-details): high, detail to detail, static, 3 s, out by cut.
- `why` (spoken selfie, no named pattern): high, medium to medium, static, 6 s, out by cut.
- `shopfront` (sign-tilt-down): low, medium to medium, tilt-down, 4 s, out by cut.

## Notes
- Every item shown gets its price, as a caption near the tag or said aloud; prices are the real tag prices.
- Other shoppers stay out of frame, and mirrors are checked for reflections before recording.
- Any 'worth more online' comparison in a caption must be a real, current listing; none is scripted.
- Label and tag share the high detail framing on purpose; the whip is the change.

## Alternatives considered
- A price comparison hook: kept out of the default captions because the number must be true.
- Two finds: one keeps it under 25 s and leaves the label and tag room to read.
