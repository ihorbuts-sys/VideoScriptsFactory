# shop-budget-challenge: Budget challenge

Kinds: shopping. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 7 steps, 28 s.

## Idea
A spending challenge: a fixed budget gives the video a goal, three picks give it a count, and the till
gives it an ending. Prices are the plot.

## Audience moment
Someone on a budget deciding whether this shop is good value. They want real prices on real items
and a total.

## Why this order
1. `budget`: the budget is the hook and the goal in one line.
2. `pick-one`: pick one, price on the tag.
3. `pick-two`: pick two, a different angle and a shelf move.
4. `pick-three`: pick three on the counter.
5. `till`: the total on the till screen, the payoff.
6. `haul`: all three together.
7. `verdict`: the verdict.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `budget` (spoken selfie, no named pattern): high, medium to medium, static, 5 s, out by cut.
- `pick-one` (pov-pick): pov, medium to close, push-in, 3 s, out by cut.
- `pick-two` (rail-truck): eye-level, medium to medium, truck-right, 4 s, out by whip.
- `pick-three` (whip-between-details): high, close to close, static, 3 s, out by cut.
- `till` (counter-over-shoulder): over-shoulder, medium to detail, push-in, 4 s, out by pull-back-reveal.
- `haul` (flat-lay-reveal): top-down, detail to wide, pull-out, 4 s, out by cut.
- `verdict` (spoken selfie, no named pattern): high, medium to medium, static, 5 s, out by cut.

## Notes
- Every item shown gets its price, as a caption near the tag or said aloud; prices are the real tag prices.
- Other shoppers stay out of frame, and mirrors are checked for reflections before recording.
- The budget amount in the lines is the creator's own premise; they say their own number.
- The cashier is asked before the over-shoulder shot, and the till screen shows only this sale.

## Alternatives considered
- Pricing everything in one shot at the end: viewers keep score as the picks come, so each pick has its
  own price caption.
- An outfit try-on of the haul: the try-on script covers it; the flat-lay keeps this one under 30 s.
