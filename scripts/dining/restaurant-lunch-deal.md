# restaurant-lunch-deal: Lunch deal

Kinds: dining. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 6 steps, 28 s. New (the offer script).

## Idea
The weekday lunch special as a value proof: the plate, what the deal is, the price on the menu, everything that
comes with it, how fast it came, and the Loca offer.

## Audience moment
An office worker at 11:30 deciding where to eat within a lunch break. They decide on price, speed and portion
(dining.md: value for money 54%, 9 in 10 weigh price [F14] [F15]). The Loca offer gives a reason to go this week.

## Why these steps in this order
1. `plate`: food first.
2. `hook`: the deal said out loud.
3. `menu`: the price on the venue's own menu, proof that it is real.
4. `spread`: what the price buys, side, soup, drink.
5. `bite`: the quality and the speed in one line.
6. `offer`: the Loca offer, one sentence, last (dining.md: offer in the closing line).

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `plate`: hero-push. Low, medium to close; cut to the selfie.
- `hook`: eye-level selfie, static; cut to the back camera.
- `menu`: high, close to detail push-in until the price fills the screen; whip.
- `spread`: lands out of the whip overhead; top-down, medium, static while the plates are set down; cut.
- `bite`: eye-level selfie, medium to close push-in as the bite comes up.
- `offer`: high selfie, static. Two spoken steps in a row, allowed.
Deliberate transitions: whip (1).

## Notes
- The spoken lines avoid quoting a price or end time, since they differ per venue; the captions point
  at what is included and when the deal runs, and the creator shows the real terms.
- No step depends on the Loca offer existing; if there is none, the creator uses the second caption.

## Alternatives considered
- "Dinner for two under a price" (dining.md idea): close to date night's closing line; lunch is the more common
  deal and a different audience.
- Opening on the price: a number without food reads as an ad (dining.md: avoid hard sell [F3]).
