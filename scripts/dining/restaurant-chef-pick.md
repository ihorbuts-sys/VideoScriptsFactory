# restaurant-chef-pick: Chef's pick

Kinds: dining. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 5 steps, 26 s. Existing script ("Kitchen story"), kept and reworked.

## Idea
Ask the kitchen what to order, then show it being made: hands plating at the pass, the cook from behind, the
finished plate, the first bite, the price. The best-practice "Chef's pick" and "kitchen to table" ideas in one.

## Audience moment
A viewer who wants to know what to order at a place they already consider. "I asked the chef what to order"
is a hook they trust more than a creator's pick, and the kitchen shots show the people behind the pass
(dining.md: diners use video to see the people and the food in motion [F24]).

## Why these steps in this order
1. `hands`: food in motion first, the strongest restaurant hook.
2. `cook`: who made it and why this plate; the creator explains on the front camera.
3. `plate`: the finished dish as the hero.
4. `bite` (new): the old script went from plate to outro without anyone tasting it; a pick needs a verdict.
5. `outro`: name, price, where.

## Changes from the kit version
- Ids `hands`, `cook`, `plate`, `outro` kept; `bite` added for the reason above. Total 14 s to 26 s.
- `venueKinds` drops `bakery`: the bakery kind has its own behind-the-counter scripts, and the pass, the sauce
  and the plated main do not describe a bakery case. Kept dining only.
- `cook` was spoken by the cook ("Meet the person behind every plate"); a creator cannot script the cook's words,
  so the creator now speaks it in pip while filming over the cook's shoulder.
- `outro` "Come visit us" replaced by "Ask for the chef's pick", the line a guest can actually use.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `hands`: hands-at-work. Top-down, close, static; match-cut on the same hands.
- `cook`: counter-over-shoulder. Over-shoulder, medium to close push-in; cut.
- `plate`: hero-push. Low, close to detail push-in until sauce fills the frame; push-through.
- `bite`: eye-level selfie, starts with the fork filling the lens and pulls out to the face (opens the
  push-through); cut.
- `outro`: high selfie, static.
Deliberate transitions: match-cut, push-through (2).

## Alternatives considered
- Keeping the cook's spoken line: it depends on staff willing to talk on camera, which many kitchens refuse.
- A sizzle (steam-hold at the pan) as the opener: good, but plating reads as "made for you" and leads into the pass.
- Filming staff: every kitchen step says "with the chef's OK"; the brief forbids filming people without consent.
