# salon-fade (Fresh fade), 26 s, 5 steps

## Idea
Script idea 3 of the best-practice file: a barber cut from overgrown to fresh, the clipper line coming in, the
blend, the client checks the result. Covers barbershops (beauty-barbershop alias).

## Audience moment
Men looking for a barber nearby, often on short notice. They decide on the blend and the price. The hook line
"let's see how long this takes" sets up the time as the outcome, an outcome-first opening from [N9]; the
creator says the real time at the reveal.

## Why these steps, in this order
1. before (selfie, 5 s, spoken): overgrown sides, same spot as the after.
2. station (detail, 4 s): clippers, guards and comb; a clean station matters to first-time clients [N12].
3. line (medium, 6 s): the clipper climbing the side, the satisfying middle of every fade video. The phone is
   propped on the mirror shelf, so the creator in the chair can film himself.
4. blend (detail, 5 s): the only step that needs a helper: the barber holds the phone. The direction says so,
   as the commercial_shots rule requires. Barbers commonly film their own fades, so this is a small ask.
5. reveal (selfie, 6 s, spoken): same spot as the before, head turned side to side, price and booking.

## Shot plan
- before: eye-level medium static, cut (camera flips).
- station: high, close push-in to detail on the clipper blade, cut to eye-level medium (two-size step and an
  angle change).
- line: hands-at-work variant at eye level, medium static; match-cut on the side of the head.
- blend: orbit-hero from high, close, orbit-right, ear to ear; cut (camera flips to the front camera).
- reveal: eye-level medium static, the same size and light as the before.
One deliberate transition (match-cut from the side in progress to the side finished).

## Alternatives considered
- Mirror reveal with a hand mirror: the phone ends up in the reflection; the orbit shows the back better.
- A barber-as-creator version (the barber films a client): needs client consent on camera; the client-as-creator
  version works without it.
- A hot-towel steam-hold step: not every shop does it; can be added as a variant.
