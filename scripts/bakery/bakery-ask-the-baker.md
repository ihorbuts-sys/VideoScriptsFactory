# bakery-ask-the-baker: Ask the baker

Kinds: bakery. `featuresMenuItem: false`. 5 steps, 25 s. New.

## Idea
Meet the baker: their hands at the bench, one question ("what do you eat after your shift?"), their pick from
the case, the inside, and the creator's verdict and price.

## Audience moment
Viewers who choose places by the people behind them, and regulars who always order the same thing. The baker's
pick gives a reason to try something new; videos with people keep the shop's energy
(best_practices/bakery.md, "Ask the baker").

## Why this order
1. `hands`: the craft first, and the person, without a face yet (hands-at-work).
2. `ask`: one question, spoken by the creator with the baker in frame.
3. `answer`: the baker's hand leads to the pick, which the viewer has not seen yet.
4. `inside`: the proof, torn open.
5. `verdict`: did the baker's pick beat the creator's usual, and what it costs.

## Shot plan as a sequence
- `hands`: top-down, close, static, the baker scoring or shaping. Cut (top-down to eye level).
- `ask`: eye-level, medium, static on the baker across the counter, creator in the pip window. Cut (to pov).
- `answer`: pov-pick, medium to close push-in as the pick comes out. Match-cut on the same pastry.
- `inside`: eye-level, close, static, torn toward the lens. Cut, camera flips.
- `verdict`: static selfie from slightly above, medium.
Deliberate transitions: one match-cut.

## Consent and voices
- The baker is on camera in `hands` and `ask`; the first direction says to ask first.
- Only the creator's line is on the prompter; the baker's answer is their own words, and the `answer` captions
  are lines the creator voices later.

## Alternatives considered
- A two-person selfie for the question: harder to frame and both faces at the edge of the frame.
- Asking "what should I order?": the shift question gets a more personal answer than the bestseller.
