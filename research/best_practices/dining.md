# Restaurant (`dining`): best practices for short venue videos

Kind: sit-down restaurants, including family restaurants (slugs `dining`, `food-and-drinks`,
`food-and-drinks-family`; 304 / 97 / 5 venues). Sources in `../sources.md`.

## Why video matters for this kind

- 58% of US TikTok users have visited a restaurant after seeing it on TikTok; 56% travelled farther than usual
  and 52% spent more [F13].
- What drives the visit: food looked appetizing 53%, looked like a fun place with friends 45%, a unique menu
  item 40%, buzz 38%, trust in the poster 33% [F13]. A script should hit the first three.
- 85% check the menu online before trying a new restaurant; 54% look for value for money [F14]. Pictures of food
  sway nearly 3 in 4 orders, and 9 in 10 weigh price [F15].
- Diners use video to decide before they go: atmosphere, food in motion, the people behind the pass [F24].

## Platform ground rules

- Vertical 9:16, sound on, people on camera, DIY look; make native content, not a resized ad [F1] [F3].
- Theme in the first 3 s, hook within 6 s [F1] [F2]. Core message in 2 to 3 s; payoff must match the hook [F11].
- Text short and out of the bottom and right UI zone [F1] [F12].

## Hooks that hold the first second

- Food in motion: a sizzle, the plating, a cheese pull, a sauce poured at the table [F24].
- A guest's first bite as the opening frame [F24].
- The dish arriving at the table, shot from the diner's seat (POV) (judgement).
- Opening lines (judgement, on the theme-first rule [F2] [F11]):
  - "This is the dish everyone in [neighbourhood] orders here."
  - "Dinner for two for forty dollars. Let's see what that gets you."
  - "I asked the chef what to order. He said this."
  - "Handmade pasta, eight dollars at lunch."
  - "Kid-friendly and the food is actually good. Here's the proof."
- Proof-first opener: the full table spread, then "here's what we ordered" [F11].

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Room, dish, bite, verdict (5 steps, about 30 s); the core restaurant shape (see `../venue_kinds.md`)
   1. Close: the hero dish arriving or being plated (4 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line at the table (4 s).
   3. Wide: the room, the light, how full it is (4 s).
   4. Detail: the bite or the cut, then the reaction (5 s).
   5. Selfie: verdict, dish name, price, venue name, where (6 s).
2. Kitchen to table (5 steps, about 30 s)
   1. Close: the pan or grill, sizzle sound (4 s).
   2. Medium: the cook plating (5 s).
   3. POV: the plate carried out to the table (4 s).
   4. Close: first bite and reaction (5 s).
   5. Selfie: name, price, "get the [dish]" (6 s).
   - Kitchen tours and the team at work are recurring restaurant formats [F24].
3. What we ordered (6 steps, about 35 s)
   1. Detail: the full table, top-down (3 s).
   2. Close: dish one, bite, a score (6 s).
   3. Close: dish two, bite, a score (6 s).
   4. Close: dessert or drink (5 s).
   5. Wide: the room (4 s).
   6. Selfie: the bill total, the must-order, name (6 s).
   - Dish-by-dish ratings are the format of the most-followed small-restaurant reviewer [F17] [F18].
4. Family table (5 steps, about 30 s); for `food-and-drinks-family`
   1. Wide: the family at the table (4 s).
   2. Close: the kids menu and the kids plate (5 s).
   3. Detail: high chair, crayons, play corner if there is one (4 s).
   4. Close: the parents' dish, bite (5 s).
   5. Selfie: total for the family, name, where (6 s).
   - Parents notice high chairs, boosters, lidded cups and the kids menu; photos of happy kids work [F25].

## Length and pacing

- Whole video 25 to 40 s: a restaurant needs a room shot and a dish shot, so it runs longer than a cafe
  (judgement); stay under 30 s when only one dish is shown [F10].
- Per shot 3 to 6 s; a bite plus reaction needs 4 to 5 s (judgement).
- Cut on the action: plate set down, fork in, first chew (judgement).
- Keep the sound of the room and the pan; sound-on platforms [F1] [F3].

## Name, location, price

- Dish name in the creator's words, using the menu's own name (judgement; see the README rule on real words).
- Price of the dish or the bill total said out loud: value is what diners search for [F14] [F15].
- Venue name spoken in the verdict and on screen at the end, not in the first frame (judgement).
- Neighbourhood in the spoken line; reservations or walk-in in one short phrase (judgement).
- Loca offer in the closing line, one sentence (judgement).

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The dish they would order and the portion size [F13] [F15].
- The menu and prices [F14] [F15].
- The room: lighting, noise, how full, date night or group table [F13] [F24].
- The people: cook, server, owner [F24].
- For families: kids menu, high chairs, space for a stroller [F25].
- Wait time and whether to book (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Polished, ad-looking footage and hard-sell lines; audiences dislike pushy advertising [F3].
- Intros before the food; the first frame must be food or a face with the hook [F11].
- A hook that oversells ("best in the city") the footage does not prove [F11].
- Covering the dish with text, or placing text in the bottom UI zone [F12].
- Showing six dishes without a single verdict: one clear pick per video (judgement).
- Filming other diners' faces up close without asking (judgement).

## Script ideas

- First visit: walk in, room, the dish everyone orders, bite, verdict and price.
- Signature dish: kitchen to table for the one dish the venue is known for [F24].
- Chef's pick: ask the cook or server what to order, order it, rate it.
- What we ordered: a table of three or four dishes, score each, name the must-order [F17].
- Dinner for two under a price: show the full bill and what it bought [F14].
- Family table: kids menu, high chair, the parents' dish, family total [F25].
- Lunch deal or offer: the Loca deal or lunch special, what you get, until when.
- Meet the owner: one line from the owner on the dish, then the bite [F24].
