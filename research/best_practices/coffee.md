# Cafe (`coffee`): best practices for short venue videos

Kind: coffee shops and cafes (production slug `coffee`, 21 venues). Sources in `../sources.md`.

## Platform ground rules that shape every cafe script

- Vertical 9:16, sound on, people on camera, a DIY look over studio polish [F1] [F3] [F8].
- The theme must be clear in the first 3 s; TikTok sees the hook window as up to 6 s [F1] [F2].
- Watch time and sends drive Reels reach to non-followers, so a video a friend would forward ("we should go
  here") beats one that is only liked [F6] (judgement on the "we should go" framing).
- On-screen text at 5 to 10 words per second at most; keep it above the bottom fifth and off the right edge,
  where the app UI sits [F1] [F12].
- Originals are favoured over reposts; no other app's watermark [F5] [F7].

## Hooks that hold the first second

- Open on motion, not on the door: espresso running from the portafilter, milk poured into a cup, ice dropping
  into cold brew, a matcha whisk [F19].
- Text-line hook in the creator's own words, from [F19]: "Here is what locals order at 8 am."
- POV openings: from behind the counter during the morning rush, or the customer's hand receiving the cup [F19].
- Opening lines that state the theme at once [F2] [F11] (examples are judgement, built on those rules):
  - "This is the best oat flat white I've had in [city]."
  - "A five dollar latte that's worth it? Let's check."
  - "If you need a place to work for three hours, this is it."
  - "They make the syrups in-house. Watch."
- Pattern interrupt that pays off right away: a tight shot of latte art that pulls back to the table [F11].
- Do not open on the logo, the sign or "Hi guys, today I'm at..." (judgement, consistent with [F11]).

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Order and verdict (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. Close: the drink being made, pour or espresso stream (3 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line with the cup in hand (4 s).
   3. Detail: first sip, then the crema or the art (3 s).
   4. Wide: the room, seating, light, who is there (4 s).
   5. Selfie or close: verdict, name, price, where (5 s).
2. Walk-in tour (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. POV: push through the door (3 s).
   2. Medium: pastry case and menu board (4 s).
   3. Close: barista making the order (4 s).
   4. Wide: the seat by the window, laptop or friends (4 s).
   5. Selfie: "Here's why I come back" plus name (5 s).
3. Signature drink build (4 steps, about 20 s)
   1. Detail: the finished drink, top-down (2 s).
   2. Close: the build in reverse order, syrup, shot, milk (8 s).
   3. Close: sip and reaction (3 s).
   4. Medium: menu board with the drink and price, name on screen (5 s).
4. Laptop or study spot test (5 steps, about 30 s)
   1. Selfie: "Can you actually work here?" (3 s).
   2. Detail: outlets, wifi sign (4 s).
   3. Wide: tables and noise level (4 s).
   4. Close: drink and a pastry (4 s).
   5. Selfie: score and the best seat (5 s).
- The order sequences mirror the four content pillars [F19]: barista stories, drink visuals, community, specials.

## Length and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 30 s; Shorts creators cite under 30 s as the best length for views and shares [F10].
- Per shot 2 to 5 s; pours and art can run 6 to 8 s because the motion itself holds attention (judgement).
- Cut on the action (the pour lands, the cup is set down) rather than on a pause (judgement).
- Keep machine sounds: grinder, steam wand, milk hitting the cup; natural sound reads as real [F3] [F19].

## Name, location, price

- Name: spoken once in the verdict step and on screen in the last 5 s; not in the first second (judgement).
- Location: neighbourhood or street in the spoken line ("on Main by the library"); city and neighbourhood words
  help local discovery [F19].
- Price: say the drink price; 54% of diners search for value for money before choosing [F14].
- Offer: a Loca offer goes in the closing line, one sentence, with what you get ("free pastry with any latte")
  (judgement).
- Give address and hours in the caption or the end card [F19].

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The drink they would order, made in front of them [F19].
- The menu board with prices; 85% check a menu before trying a new place [F14].
- Seating: how many tables, outlets, light, is it quiet (judgement; the laptop-test format is common on
  coffee TikTok).
- The pastry case, since most cafe orders pair a drink with food (judgement).
- The people: barista and regulars; videos with no people lose the shop's energy [F19].
- How busy it is and when (morning line vs. calm afternoon) (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Overproduced, heavily filtered clips; natural light reads better [F3] [F19].
- Hard-sell lines ("Come visit us today!") and notice-style text [F19] [F20].
- Empty-room footage with no people [F19].
- Long intros before the first drink shot [F11].
- Text under the caption bar or behind the like column [F12].
- Claims the video does not show ("best coffee in town" with no reason); the payoff must match the hook
  [F11] (judgement on wording).

## Script ideas

- First visit: walk in, order the house favourite, sip, honest verdict and price.
- Signature drink: the build of the drink the cafe is known for, close-up, then the first sip.
- Behind the bar: the barista's morning setup before opening, first shot pulled [F19].
- What locals order: ask the barista or a regular what they get, order it, rate it [F19].
- Work spot test: outlets, wifi, noise, best seat, a score out of ten.
- Seasonal drink: the limited drink of the month and its price, before it is gone [F19].
- Drink and pastry pairing: the barista picks a pastry for your drink, you taste both.
- Offer: the Loca deal shown at the counter, what you get and until when.
