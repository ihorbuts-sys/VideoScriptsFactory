# Best practices: beauty-salon (Salon)

Covers hair salons, colorists, barbers, nail studios, brow and lash bars, skin and facial rooms.
Sources: `../sources.md` (numbers N1 to N41). "(judgement)" marks our own reasoning.

## Platform baseline that applies to every salon video

- Vertical 9:16, at least 720p, keep text and faces inside the UI safe zone [N1].
- State what the video is about within the first 3 s; land the hook within the first 6 s [N1].
- Instagram: Mosseri calls the first 3 s the hook; it needs attention, not bait or a cliffhanger [N5].
- Instagram ranks Reels on watch time, likes per reach and sends; sends matter most for new viewers [N5].
- Recycled clips with another app's watermark are shown less in recommendations; your own logo is fine [N8].
- YouTube Shorts: phone-shot is enough; catch attention in the first seconds [N6]; Shorts run up to 3 min [N7].
- On-screen text: 5 to 10 words per second at most [N1]; one line naming the service works for nails [N11].
- Creator, staff or client on camera, unpolished UGC look, end on a clear call to action [N1].

## Hooks that hold the first second

The reveal is the payoff, so the first second must promise it without spending it [N9][N39].

Opening shots:
- The "before" in harsh honesty: grown-out roots, chipped polish, overgrown fade, held for under 1 s [N9].
- A 0.5 s flash of the finished look, then cut back to the chair ("here is where we started") [N9] (judgement on length).
- POV from the chair: phone at eye level looking down at the cut [N10].
- Macro of one finished accent nail, glossy topcoat filling the frame [N11].
- Foil or cape coming off, mid-motion, so the frame moves in frame one (judgement).

Opening lines (creator speaks, 8 to 14 words):
- "I let a stranger fix my roots, here is the result."
- "First time at [Venue], and I asked for something I have never tried."
- "This fade took 25 minutes, watch the line come in."
- "I showed her one photo and said do this to my nails."
- "Booked the cheapest brow appointment on [Street], was it worth it?"
- Outcome-first lines work: state the result in the first second, then build to it [N9].

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Before / process / reveal (the default) [N9][N14][N38]
   - Before in clean, even light (2 to 3 s) -> 3 to 5 fast process cuts, one example per step, no repeats
     (1 to 2 s each) [N39] -> reveal with a slow turn or mirror (3 to 4 s) [N38] -> name, price, booking (2 to 3 s).
   - Same light, angle and background for before and after so the comparison is honest [N9][N39].
2. POV in the chair [N10]
   - Walk in and sit (2 s) -> POV of the stylist's hands at work (3 x 2 s) -> creator reacts on selfie (3 s)
     -> mirror reveal (3 s) -> "booked through [link], [price]" (2 s).
3. Rescue story [N9]
   - Problem close-up ("watch her fix this") (2 s) -> stylist explains the plan in one line (3 s) -> process
     glimpses (3 x 1.5 s) -> reveal held short so viewers rewatch (judgement) -> booking line.
4. Nails two-shot [N11]
   - Hand turns slowly, about 3 s per angle on a plain light background -> macro hold on one nail -> overlay
     "Full set, gel-x, 90 minutes" style label -> price and booking.

## Lengths and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 35 s fits a single reveal; keep TikToks under 60 s so viewers finish them [N9].
- Transformation and service-business clips land best under 30 s [N14].
- Process shots 1 to 2 s each; before 2 to 3 s; reveal 3 to 4 s; nails 3 s per angle [N11][N39] (judgement on exact values).
- Cut on movement (a brush stroke, a cape swing) to hide time jumps (judgement).

## Where name, location and offer appear

- Say the service and the city aloud: TikTok transcribes audio for search; add a location and service overlay [N14].
- Use local, specific words ("balayage in Denver") over vague ones ("new color today") [N14].
- Price: clients want costs before they reach out; price and location are the top two salon choice factors [N9][N12].
- End card: venue name, street, price of the service shown, "book on Loca" (judgement).
- Every video carries a booking prompt; the link goes straight to booking, one tap to a slot [N14][N11].

## What first-time clients want to see

- The price of the exact service filmed [N12].
- The person who does the work, talking to camera for a line or two [N9][N1].
- A clean, hygienic station: hygiene ranks first among reasons clients pay more [N12].
- A real result in honest light, not a filter [N9][N39].
- How to book and how soon a slot is open; 45% prefer booking digitally to see open slots [N12].
- Reviews: 78% of salon and spa regulars check reviews before a visit [N13]; a short spoken review helps (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Filters or different lighting between before and after; reads as fake [N9][N39].
- Long process sections with repeated steps; show each step once [N39].
- Filming other clients in the background without their consent; ask before filming or posting any client [N9].
- A bottle-and-shiny-hair product shot as the whole idea; it no longer works as a strategy [N41].
- Ads: Meta allows before/after for general cosmetic products and procedures, but bans body-shaming messaging
  and requires 18+ targeting for cosmetic procedures [N2]; TikTok bans before/after imagery that exaggerates
  results [N4]. Organic Loca posts should follow the same line: real, unexaggerated results (judgement).
- Skin treatments that border on medical (peels, injectables): no claims about curing conditions; refer to
  the `sport_health` rules (judgement).
- Paid or gifted visits: the creator must disclose the incentive where the review appears [N21].

## Script ideas

1. First visit: creator books the entry service, shows price at the desk, POV in the chair, mirror reveal.
2. Transformation: before in window light, three process cuts, reveal turn, stylist names the technique.
3. Fade in 25 seconds: barber's clipper line coming in, close on the blend, client checks the mirror.
4. Nail set close-up: hand turns at three angles, macro hold, service name and time on screen, price.
5. Meet the stylist: one line on what they love doing, hands at work, their signature look, how to book.
6. Brow or lash before/after: tight eye-level close-ups in the same light, one process cut, reveal.
7. Quick tip from the chair: stylist gives one aftercare tip while finishing, creator repeats it to camera.
8. Offer: the first-visit price on screen, the station, the result, "book on Loca this week".
