# Going out (`entertainment`): best practices for short venue videos

Kind: bars, breweries, lounges, live music, and activity venues such as laser tag, waterparks, adventure parks
and casinos (slug `entertainment`, aliases `craft-beer`, `events-music-n-film`, `events-social`; 20 venues plus
7 craft-beer). Two families of script: a night out (drink, room, music) and an activity (the thing you do).
Sources in `../sources.md`.

## Why video matters for this kind

- "Looked like a fun place to go with friends" moves 45% of TikTok users who visited a venue after a video, the
  second reason after appetizing food [F13]. For this kind the room and the crowd are the product (judgement).
- Drink-making clips are the bar staple; keep them under 30 s [F27]. A bar director calls 30 to 90 s drink
  videos "instantly gratifying" [F29].
- Staff personality sells: short staff skits launched a Denver bar's new cocktail menu [F28]; staff spotlights
  build trust [F27].
- For activity venues the signature attraction is the shareable draw: one creator's POV of a single slide drew
  286M views [F30]. Show the fun itself, the team, and guests tagging friends [F31].

## Platform ground rules

- Vertical 9:16, sound on, people on camera, DIY look [F1] [F3].
- Theme in the first 3 s, hook within 6 s [F1] [F2]; core message in 2 to 3 s [F11].
- Sends drive reach to non-followers [F6]: "tag who you'd bring" is the natural share for this kind
  (judgement; tag-a-friend asks are a named FEC tactic [F31]).
- Text short, out of the bottom and right UI zone [F1] [F12].

## Hooks that hold the first second

- Night out:
  - The pour: a beer pulled from the tap with the head forming, a cocktail shaken and strained [F27] [F29].
  - The garnish or flame moment of a signature drink (judgement).
  - The room at its loudest: band on stage, crowd singing (judgement; live music highlights are a named
    format [F27]).
  - Lines (judgement, on the theme-first rule [F2] [F11]):
    - "Six dollar pints until seven. This is the spot."
    - "They brew everything behind that glass wall."
    - "Jazz, no cover, on a Tuesday."
    - "I let the bartender pick my drink."
- Activity:
  - POV of the ride or the run: the drop of a slide, the zipline start, the laser tag arena door opening [F30].
  - The reaction: a friend's face on the drop, a scoreboard reveal (judgement).
  - Lines (judgement):
    - "We tried the tallest slide in the park."
    - "Laser tag, four of us, here's who won."
    - "Twenty dollars and two hours of this."

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Drink, room, verdict (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. Close: the signature drink made or poured (5 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line with the glass (4 s).
   3. Wide: the room, the bar, the crowd (4 s).
   4. Detail: sip, the glass on the bar, the menu with the price (4 s).
   5. Selfie: verdict, name, happy hour or price, where (6 s).
2. Brewery walk (5 steps, about 30 s)
   1. Wide: the tanks or the brewhouse (4 s).
   2. Close: the tap list (4 s).
   3. Close: the flight poured (6 s).
   4. Medium: friends at the table, food truck or games if there are some (5 s).
   5. Selfie: favourite beer, price, name (6 s).
3. Night arc (6 steps, about 35 s)
   1. POV: walking in, music audible (3 s).
   2. Close: first drink (4 s).
   3. Wide: the stage or the dance floor (6 s).
   4. Medium: friends, a toast (4 s).
   5. Detail: the event board or the schedule (4 s).
   6. Selfie: what night to come, cover charge, name (6 s).
4. Activity run (5 steps, about 30 s)
   1. POV: the ride, the arena or the course, mid-action (5 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line, out of breath or soaked (4 s).
   3. Wide: the whole park or arena, how big it is (4 s).
   4. Medium: friends or kids reacting, the scoreboard (5 s).
   5. Selfie: ticket price, how long you stayed, name (6 s).

## Length and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 35 s; drink clips under 30 s [F27] [F10]; a night arc or an activity can run to 35 s
  (judgement).
- Per shot 2 to 6 s; POV action up to 6 s because the motion holds attention (judgement).
- Cut to the beat of the room's music where possible; sound is on [F1] (judgement on cutting to the beat).
- Low light: shoot near the bar lights and keep shots short (judgement).

## Name, location, price

- Name spoken in the verdict, on screen at the end (judgement).
- Price: pint, cocktail, cover or ticket said out loud; diners search for value [F14].
- When matters as much as where: happy hour times, the music night, the event date [F27].
- Location by neighbourhood or landmark (judgement).
- Loca offer in the closing line, one sentence (judgement).

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The crowd and the vibe at the time they would go [F13].
- The drinks menu or tap list with prices (judgement, as with food menus [F14]).
- Music, stage, games, events, and which night [F27].
- For activities: the main attraction, the queue, age or height limits, ticket price, how long a visit takes
  (judgement).
- Food: kitchen, snacks or a food truck outside (judgement).
- Parking and how to get home late (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Heavy drinking on camera, shots lined up, drunk guests: it reads badly and can breach platform rules on
  alcohol (judgement).
- Close-ups of strangers' faces without asking (judgement).
- Polished promo cuts and hard-sell lines [F3].
- A dark, muddy room with no subject; start on a lit drink or a face [F11] (judgement on lighting).
- Long walk-in intros before the drink or the ride [F11].
- Activity safety moments played for laughs (judgement).

## Script ideas

- Bartender's pick: let the bartender choose a drink, watch it made, taste it [F27].
- Happy hour run: what the happy hour buys, the times, one drink and one bite [F27].
- Brewery flight: the brewhouse, the tap list, a flight, the favourite.
- Live music night: arrive, first drink, the band, which night and the cover [F27].
- Meet the staff: one line from the bartender or owner, then their signature drink [F27] [F28].
- Activity first run: POV of the main attraction, reaction, ticket price [F30].
- Group night: four friends, a game or a match, who won, tag who you'd bring [F31].
- Offer or event: the Loca deal or this week's event, what you get, when.
