# Quick bites (`fast-food`): best practices for short venue videos

Kind: fast food counters, pizza by the slice, taco and burger spots, food trucks (slugs `fast-food`,
`food-truck`; 19 / 23 venues). Sources in `../sources.md`.

## Why this kind is filmed apart from dining

- The story is speed, price and one handheld item eaten standing or in the car, not a room and a table
  (judgement, recorded in `../venue_kinds.md`).
- The best-known small-restaurant review format is takeout eaten in the car, each item rated 1 to 10 on camera
  [F17]; its honest scores send lines to small venues [F18].
- Fast food creators are a named genre: reviews, "eating what employees eat", odd combos; 94% of 13 to 39 year
  olds visited a fast food chain in the past month, and these videos make viewers crave cheap eats [F16].
- 54% of diners look for value for money; 31% say a price rise significantly changes their choice (40% of
  Gen Z) [F14]. Price on camera matters more here than in any other food kind (judgement).

## Platform ground rules

- Vertical 9:16, sound on, people on camera, DIY look [F1] [F3].
- Theme in the first 3 s, hook within 6 s [F1] [F2]; core message in 2 to 3 s [F11].
- Text short, out of the bottom and right UI zone [F1] [F12].

## Hooks that hold the first second

- The item held up to the lens, then the first bite (judgement; a filmed bite is the classic food hook [F24]).
- The build: a burger stacked or a burrito rolled, cut fast; prep from start to final product is a food truck
  format [F26] (the time-lapse treatment is judgement).
- The cheese pull on a slice, the sauce squeeze, the fry basket lifted from oil (judgement).
- The truck window: the order handed out to the camera (judgement).
- Opening lines (judgement, on the theme-first rule [F2] [F11]):
  - "Twelve dollars. Burger, fries and a drink. Worth it?"
  - "This truck has a line every Friday. Here's why."
  - "The fastest lunch near [landmark], and it's good."
  - "I asked what the cooks eat on shift."
  - "Rating everything on the menu under ten dollars."

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Order, bite, rate (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. Close: the item held to the camera (3 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line with the price (4 s).
   3. Detail: first bite, cross-section (4 s).
   4. Medium: menu board with prices (4 s).
   5. Selfie: score out of ten, name, where (6 s).
   - Mirrors the try-and-rate structure [F17].
2. Window to car (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. POV: walking up to the truck or counter, the line (4 s).
   2. Medium: ordering at the window, menu visible (4 s).
   3. Close: the order handed over (3 s).
   4. Close: unwrap and bite in the car or on the curb (5 s).
   5. Selfie: verdict, total, where it parks today (6 s).
3. The build (4 steps, about 20 s)
   1. Close: grill, griddle or fryer, sound up (4 s).
   2. Close: the item assembled, fast cuts (6 s).
   3. Detail: bite and inside (4 s).
   4. Selfie: name, price, hours (6 s).
   - Prep from start to final product is a named food truck format [F26].
4. Menu run under a budget (6 steps, about 35 s)
   1. Selfie: "Ten dollars, how much can I get?" (4 s).
   2. Medium: the menu board (4 s).
   3. Close: item one, bite, score (6 s).
   4. Close: item two, bite, score (6 s).
   5. Detail: the receipt (3 s).
   6. Selfie: best item, total, name (6 s).

## Length and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 30 s; under 30 s performs best on Shorts [F10]. Fast cuts suit fast food (judgement).
- Per shot 2 to 5 s; builds can be sped up (YouTube and TikTok cameras run 0.3x to 3x [F9]).
- Cut on the bite and on the hand-over (judgement).
- Keep the fryer, griddle and wrapper sounds [F1] [F3].

## Name, location, price

- Price spoken in the hook or the first selfie line, and the total at the end (judgement, from [F14] [F16]).
- Name spoken in the verdict, on screen at the end (judgement).
- Location: for a counter, the cross street or landmark; for a truck, where it parks and which days, which changes
  (judgement; showing how the truck picks its spots is a named format [F26]).
- Hours and how long the wait was, in one phrase (judgement).
- Loca offer in the last line, one sentence (judgement).

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The item in a hand, so the size is clear (judgement).
- The menu board with prices [F14] [F15].
- The line and how fast it moves (judgement).
- Where to eat it: tables, a counter, or takeaway only (judgement).
- For trucks: where it is today and the hours (judgement, from the location formats in [F26]).
- Dietary options where they exist (vegan, gluten-free), said in one phrase (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Ad-style claims and polished footage [F3].
- A long walk-up before the food appears; the item must be on screen first [F11].
- Hiding the price or saying "affordable" instead of a number (judgement).
- A score that does not match the reaction; honest verdicts are the format's value [F17] [F18] (judgement on wording).
- Filming staff or the line without asking (judgement).

## Script ideas

- Order and rate: the most popular item, first bite, score out of ten [F17].
- Window to car: walk up, order, eat in the car, verdict and total.
- The build: the signature item assembled on the griddle, then the bite [F26].
- Under ten dollars: how much food a set budget buys, item by item.
- What the cooks eat: ask staff for their shift order, try it [F16].
- Find the truck: where it parks today, the line, the order, the hours [F26].
- Combo hack: an off-menu combo or extra the staff recommends [F16].
- Offer: the Loca deal at the counter, what you get and when.
