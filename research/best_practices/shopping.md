# Best practices: shopping (Shop)

Covers boutiques, clothing shops, thrift and vintage, music shops, gift and book shops, knick-knacks.
Sources: `../sources.md` (numbers N1 to N41). "(judgement)" marks our own reasoning.
The common shape: a rail or shelf, one find, the find in use (a try-on or a demo), the price.

## Platform baseline

- Vertical 9:16, 720p or better, text inside the UI safe zone [N1].
- Proposition in 3 s, hook within 6 s, sound or music, transitions to hold attention, clear CTA [N1].
- Instagram: first 3 s are the hook; sends drive reach to new viewers [N5]; own logo is fine [N8].
- Viewers trust a friend's tip more than a hard sell [N26]; TikTok points brands at behind-the-scenes and
  street-interview style content to build trust [N36].

## Hooks that hold the first second

Opening shots:
- The find held up to the lens, tag visible (thrift and vintage) [N27].
- Hand pulling one piece off a full rail, mid-motion (judgement).
- Mirror shot already in the outfit, then cut back to the rail (judgement).
- Fingers hitting the first chord on a guitar off the wall; sound in frame one [N29].
- A wall of shelves from the door, push in toward one item (judgement).

Opening lines (creator, 8 to 14 words):
- "Found this jacket at [Venue] for 18 dollars, look at the lining."
- "Everything I would buy at [Venue] with 50 dollars."
- "I asked the owner to dress me in their favourite pieces."
- "This shop on [Street] has a whole wall of vintage denim."
- "I can barely play, but this guitar made me sound good."
- Price-comparison hooks work: a cheap find set against what it sells for elsewhere [N27] (use only true
  numbers, see below).

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Try-on [N25][N26]
   - Rail, hand picks a piece (2 s) -> close on fabric and tag with price (2 s) -> changing-room curtain (1 s)
     -> try-on reveal in the mirror, one turn showing fit and colour (3 to 4 s) [N26] -> second piece, quicker
     (3 s) -> creator verdict to camera (4 s) -> shop name, street, price range.
2. Find of the day (thrift, vintage, gifts) [N27]
   - The find held up (2 s) -> where it was in the shop (2 s) -> detail close: label, stitching, maker's mark
     (2 x 2 s) -> the price tag (2 s) -> creator says why it is a find (4 s) -> how to get there.
3. Demo (music shops) [N29][N30]
   - Wall of instruments (2 s) -> staff member picks one and plays a few bars (4 s) -> close on hands and the
     headstock or brand (2 s) -> creator tries it (3 s) -> staff tip in one line (4 s) -> price and shop.
4. Budget challenge (judgement)
   - "50 dollars at [Venue]" on screen (2 s) -> three picks, price on each (3 x 3 s) -> the total at the till
     (3 s) -> the outfit or haul together (3 s) -> shop name.

## Lengths and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 35 s; a try-on with two pieces fits in 30 s (judgement).
- Product close-ups 1.5 to 2 s; reveals 3 to 4 s; spoken verdict 4 s (judgement).
- Cut the try-on on a turn or a curtain pull to hide the change (judgement).
- Text on screen per price: one line, a few words [N1].

## Where name, location and offer appear

- The shop name in the first spoken line or as text over the facade (judgement).
- Price on every item shown, as text near the tag; price is what makes a find a find [N27] (judgement).
- Street or neighbourhood said aloud and on screen; local keywords help search [N14].
- End: name, street, opening days, any Loca offer; viewers are pointed to where to buy [N25].

## What first-time visitors want to see

- Real prices and the price range of the shop (judgement).
- What the shop stocks: styles, sizes, brands, whether it is hand-picked or a dig (judgement).
- Fit and colour on a real body, not on a hanger [N26].
- The owner or staff and how they help: a styling tip, a demo [N29][N25].
- The real shop and the real tag, not a staged find: viewers call out staged thrift finds [N28].
- Opening hours, location, whether there is parking or a shop entrance off the street (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Staged or inflated finds: viewers notice and call it out [N28]; any "worth 800 dollars online" comparison must
  be a real, current listing (judgement).
- Other shoppers in the frame, especially in changing-room areas; never film inside a cubicle other than the
  creator's own, and keep mirrors from catching people (judgement).
- Product-only slideshows with music and no person: reads as a catalogue ad (judgement, see [N1] on people).
- Brand logos and copyrighted music played over the shop speakers can trigger audio matching; record the
  voice-over in post instead (judgement).
- Gifted items must be disclosed where the review appears [N21]; rewards conditioned on a positive review are
  banned [N22].

## Script ideas

1. Find of the day: the piece held up, where it hung, the label, the tag price, why it is a find.
2. Try-on haul: two pieces from the rail, mirror turns, the price of each, creator picks one.
3. Budget challenge: a set amount, three picks with prices, total at the till, the full outfit.
4. Owner's pick: the owner pulls their favourite item and says why, creator tries it, price.
5. Shop tour: door, the main rail or wall, the corner nobody notices, the till, opening days.
6. Music shop demo: staff plays a few bars, creator tries the same instrument, one tip, price.
7. Gift under 30 dollars: three gift ideas on the counter, price on each, wrapped at the till.
8. New in this week: the delivery opened, three new pieces, prices, "on the rail now".
