# Best practices: sport_health (Fitness and wellness)

Covers gyms, yoga and pilates studios, sauna studios, massage, IV bars, skin clinics, a dental clinic.
Sources: `../sources.md` (numbers N1 to N41). "(judgement)" marks our own reasoning.
The rule that shapes every script here: show the place, the person and the session; never promise a body,
health or medical outcome.

## Platform baseline

- Vertical 9:16, 720p or better, content inside the UI safe zone [N1].
- Proposition in the first 3 s, hook within 6 s [N1]; Instagram treats the first 3 s as the hook [N5].
- Creators, staff or customers on camera, UGC look, clear call to action at the end [N1].
- Shorts are judged on the first seconds; a phone is enough [N6].
- Studios: start with movement, a bold statement or a visual surprise, or viewers scroll past [N24].

## Hooks that hold the first second

Opening shots:
- Mid-movement: a kettlebell at the top of a swing, a mat unrolling, a sauna door opening into steam [N24].
- POV walking through the door into the training floor or studio (judgement).
- The instructor looking into the lens and counting the creator in (judgement).
- A close of the setting that tells the kind at once: cedar benches, a reformer carriage, a massage table
  dressed with towels (judgement).

Opening lines (creator, 8 to 14 words):
- "I tried my first reformer class at [Venue], here is how it went."
- "What a 45 minute class at [Venue] actually looks like."
- "First time in a sauna studio, I had no idea what to bring."
- "This gym has one thing I have never seen before."
- "Walk into [Venue] with me, nobody told me about this room."
- Lines name the session and the feeling, not a result ("I sweated", not "I lost") (judgement, see [N3]).

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Try a class (default for studios and gyms) [N23][N25]
   - Arrive at the door, POV (2 s) -> front desk and the price board (2 to 3 s) -> instructor greets, one line
     to camera (3 s) -> class in motion, creator in frame, others out of frame (3 x 2 s) -> creator selfie
     after, one honest line (4 s) -> name, class price, how to book (2 s).
2. Tour [N23]
   - Facade or sign (2 s) -> pan across the main floor or studio (3 s) -> the standout kit or room (3 s) ->
     changing room and showers, empty (2 s) -> the instructor or owner says who it is for (4 s) -> offer.
3. Treatment session (massage, sauna, IV bar, facial) (judgement)
   - Reception and menu with prices (3 s) -> the room before use, empty and clean (3 s) -> practitioner explains
     what happens in one line (4 s) -> creator relaxed, hands or feet only during the treatment (2 x 2 s) ->
     creator after, a feeling not a claim ("I slept well tonight" is too much, "felt calm" is fine) -> booking.
4. Meet the team (clinics, dental) [N18][N19]
   - Waiting area with no patients (2 s) -> clinician introduces themselves to camera (4 s) -> one room and
     one piece of kit explained (3 s) -> what a first appointment includes and costs (4 s) -> booking line.

## Lengths and pacing

- Whole video 25 to 40 s: a class needs more set-up shots than a salon reveal (judgement).
- Movement shots 2 s each, cut on the top or bottom of a rep (judgement).
- Spoken steps 3 to 5 s for an 8 to 14 word line (judgement).
- Quick tip videos 15 to 30 s [N14].

## Where name, location and offer appear

- Spoken in the hook or the first spoken line: the venue and the class name (judgement).
- On screen: class or treatment name and price when the price board or menu is shown (judgement).
- Say the area and the service aloud and as text; TikTok transcribes audio for local search [N14].
- End: venue name, street, first-class or intro offer, booking prompt; link straight to the schedule [N14].

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The space: floor, studio, changing rooms, the kit they will use [N23].
- The instructor or practitioner and how they talk to a beginner (judgement).
- What one session costs and whether there is an intro offer (judgement; price is a top factor in the
  related salon study [N12]).
- That beginners belong: a mixed-level class, a first-timer on camera (judgement).
- How to book a class or slot (judgement).
- Clinics: cleanliness, who will treat them, what the first visit includes (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Filming other members: gyms ask for consent from anyone who may appear, and keep others out of frame,
  including the background [N15][N16][N17]. Agree with the venue on an empty corner, an empty class slot,
  or film only staff and the creator (judgement).
- Patients and clients in clinics: a face, voice, tattoo, room number or a screen can identify someone;
  written, specific authorization is needed, a general media release is not enough [N18][N19]. Scripts here
  film empty rooms, staff and the creator only (judgement).
- Weight and body claims: no shaming, no ideal body type, no "easy" results; weight or muscle-gain claims are
  18+ only and must not suggest results without diet or exercise [N3].
- Side-by-side body comparisons and close-ups pinching fat are banned for weight-loss ads on Meta; fitness
  services are exempt from the age rules [N2]. Do not script body before/after at all (judgement).
- Health claims for IV drips, vitamin shots or saunas: the FTC treated unsupported efficacy claims for IV and
  vitamin treatments as violations [N20]. Lines describe the session, the room and how it felt, never a cure,
  immunity, detox or recovery claim (judgement).
- Med-spa or skin procedures: TikTok requires risks and recovery times to be disclosed and bans exaggerated
  before/after [N4]; out of scope for creator scripts (judgement).
- Long shots of empty equipment with no person: reads as a property listing (judgement).
- Gifted classes must be disclosed where the review appears [N21].

## Script ideas

1. First class: door POV, price board, instructor greets, three moves, honest selfie verdict, booking.
2. Gym tour in 30 seconds: sign, floor pan, the standout machine, changing room, owner says who it is for.
3. Sauna or cold plunge first time: what to bring, the room, creator steps in, how it felt, session price.
4. Meet the instructor: one line on their style, a cue they always give, the class they teach, schedule.
5. Massage or facial booking: menu and price, the empty room, practitioner explains, calm after shot.
6. One move from the coach: the instructor shows one beginner move, creator copies it, class name and time.
7. Clinic first appointment: waiting area, clinician introduces, what the visit includes, how to book.
8. Intro offer: the first-week or first-class price on screen, three shots of the class, "book on Loca".
