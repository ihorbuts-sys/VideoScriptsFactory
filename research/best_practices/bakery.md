# Bakery (`bakery`): best practices for short venue videos

Kind: bakeries, pastry shops, donut and cake shops (production slug `bakery`, 21 venues). Sources in
`../sources.md`.

## Platform ground rules

- Vertical 9:16, sound on, real people, phone-shot over polished photography [F1] [F3] [F20].
- Theme clear in the first 3 s, hook within 6 s [F1] [F2].
- On-screen text short (5 to 10 words per second at most), kept out of the bottom and right UI zone [F1] [F12].
- Reach to new viewers rides on watch time and sends [F6]; a pastry worth sending to a friend is the target
  (judgement).

## Hooks that hold the first second

- The cut or the break: a croissant torn open to show the layers, a knife through a cake, a cream-filled donut
  squeezed (judgement; process and texture shots are the strongest bakery format [F20]).
- Fresh out of the oven: a tray pulled from the oven, steam visible [F20].
- Process close-ups: dough being laminated, macarons piped, sourdough scored [F20].
- Sound as the hook: crust cracking, the knife scraping; keep the kitchen audio [F20].
- One filmed bite of an unusual pastry can start a queue (the Paris crookie) [F22].
- Opening lines (judgement, built on the theme-first rule [F2] [F11]):
  - "They sell out of these by ten. Here's why."
  - "Listen to this crust."
  - "This croissant has more layers than I can count."
  - "Four dollars for a cinnamon roll the size of my hand."
  - "Everything in this case was made this morning."

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Break and bite (5 steps, about 25 s)
   1. Close: the pastry torn or cut, layers or filling visible (3 s).
   2. Selfie: hook line holding it (4 s).
   3. Detail: the bite, then the inside (3 s).
   4. Medium: the full case with labels and prices (5 s).
   5. Selfie: verdict, name, price, where (5 s).
2. Morning before the doors open (5 steps, about 30 s)
   1. Wide: dark shop, baker at the bench (4 s).
   2. Close: shaping or laminating dough (6 s).
   3. Detail: tray out of the oven (4 s).
   4. Medium: case filled, doors open, first customer (6 s).
   5. Selfie or close: "This is what I'd get first" plus name (5 s).
   - Based on the "day in the bakery before the first customer" idea [F21].
3. Box build (4 steps, about 25 s)
   1. Selfie: "Building a box for the office, twenty dollars" (4 s).
   2. Close: picking each item from the case, one per beat (10 s).
   3. Detail: the open box, top-down (4 s).
   4. Selfie: favourite, total price, name (6 s).
4. New or seasonal item launch (4 steps, about 20 s)
   1. Close: the item being finished, glaze or dusting (5 s).
   2. Detail: the item on the counter (3 s).
   3. Close: bite and reaction (4 s).
   4. Medium: price card, available until when, name (6 s).
   - Mirrors the launch sequence creation, first batch, reactions [F20]; limited items draw more interest [F21].

## Length and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 30 s; process clips work at 15 to 45 s [F20]; under 30 s is the Shorts sweet spot [F10].
- Watch completion: 80% completion on a short clip matters more than raw views [F20].
- Per shot 2 to 5 s; lamination or piping can hold 6 to 8 s because the repetition is satisfying (judgement).
- Cut on the tear, the bite and the tray landing (judgement).

## Name, location, price

- Name spoken in the verdict step and on screen at the end; never as the opening frame (judgement).
- Price per item, said out loud; value for money is the top thing 54% of diners search for [F14].
- Location by neighbourhood in the spoken line; address and hours in the caption (judgement, as for cafes [F19]).
- "Sells out by" time is useful information, not hype, when it is true (judgement; see [F23] on false claims).
- Offer (Loca deal) in the last spoken line, one sentence (judgement).

## What first-time visitors want to see

- The inside of the hero item: layers, filling, crumb (judgement, from [F20] process focus).
- The full case with labels and prices; 85% check a menu before trying a new place [F14].
- Size: the pastry next to a hand or a cup (judgement).
- That it is made there: hands, ovens, flour [F20]. A bakery caught reselling bought-in pastries went viral for
  the wrong reason [F23].
- When to come: what time the fresh batch lands, whether there is a line (judgement).
- Seating or takeaway only (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Notice-style text ("Fresh sourdough available today"); tell a short story or ask a question [F20].
- Designed graphics with the logo; use a native product shot [F20].
- Polished photos instead of clips [F20].
- Claims the footage does not prove ("best croissant in the state") [F11] [F23].
- Showing a pastry only from the top: the inside is what sells it (judgement).
- Long silent shots with no kitchen sound [F20].

## Script ideas

- First bite: tear the signature pastry open, bite, verdict and price.
- Morning shift: the baker's hour before opening, ending on the full case [F21].
- Fresh batch: the tray coming out of the oven and the first customer to get one [F20].
- Box for twenty dollars: build a mixed box, show the total and the favourite.
- New this week: the seasonal or limited item, how it is finished, until when it is on [F20] [F21].
- Ask the baker: one question to the person who makes it ("what do you eat here?"), then try it.
- Sell-out run: arrive early for the item that sells out, show the line, show why.
- Offer: the Loca deal at the counter, what you get and how to claim it.
