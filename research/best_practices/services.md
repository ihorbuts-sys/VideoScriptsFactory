# Best practices: services (Local pro)

Covers phone repair, tutoring, piano and music schools, cleaning, photography, pet services and small
business services. Sources: `../sources.md` (numbers N1 to N41). "(judgement)" marks our own reasoning.
The common video is a person and their work: meet the pro, watch the work, see the result, how to book.

## Platform baseline

- Vertical 9:16, 720p or better, text inside the UI safe zone [N1].
- Proposition in 3 s, hook within 6 s, staff or customers on camera, UGC look, clear CTA [N1].
- Instagram: first 3 s are the hook [N5]; YouTube: grab attention in the first seconds, a phone is enough [N6].
- Raw, unpolished clips often beat professional production for service businesses [N14].

## Hooks that hold the first second

Opening shots:
- The problem in close-up: a shattered screen, a stained sofa, a cluttered desk [N14].
- Hands mid-task: a screwdriver lifting the screen, a brush on the stain, a student's fingers on keys (judgement).
- The tool laid out on the bench, then the pro's hand picks it up (judgement).
- The finished result flashed for half a second before cutting back to the problem (judgement).
- Phone repair creators built large audiences on the repair itself as the hook: opening the device on camera
  [N31][N32].

Opening lines (creator, 8 to 14 words):
- "My screen was shattered, [Venue] said give us 40 minutes."
- "I sat in on a first piano lesson at [Venue]."
- "This is what a two hour deep clean does to a kitchen."
- "Tutors at [Venue] showed me how they start with a new student."
- "I asked [Name] what most people get wrong before a photo shoot."
- Tip-style hooks work: "three signs your repair was done right" [N14].

## Shot orders that read as a story

1. Before / process / result (repair, cleaning) [N14][N33]
   - The problem close (2 s) -> hand-over at the counter and the quoted price (3 s) -> process cuts, tools and
     hands (3 x 1.5 s) -> result next to the before angle (3 s) -> creator checks it, one line (4 s) -> name,
     time taken, how to book. Keep transformations under 30 s [N14].
2. Meet the pro [N33][N14]
   - The pro at work, wide (2 s) -> pro to camera: name and what they do, one line (4 s) -> close on the craft
     (2 x 2 s) -> one tip in their words (4 s) -> how to book and the starting price (3 s).
3. First lesson (tutoring, music school) [N34][N35]
   - Door and room (2 s) -> teacher greets, one line on how a first lesson works (4 s) -> the creator (adult)
     tries, hands on keys or a worksheet (2 x 2 s) -> a small win, first melody or a solved problem (3 s) [N35]
     -> lesson price and booking.
4. Quick tip (judgement on structure) [N14]
   - Hook line with the tip promise (3 s) -> pro shows the tip with hands (2 x 2 s) -> creator repeats it (3 s)
     -> venue name and service. 15 to 30 s total [N14].

## Lengths and pacing

- Whole video 20 to 35 s; transformation under 30 s, quick tips 15 to 30 s [N14].
- Process cuts 1 to 2 s; result hold 3 s; spoken lines 3 to 5 s (judgement).
- Time-lapse compresses long work; say the real time taken on screen (judgement, see [N14]).

## Where name, location and offer appear

- Every video ends with a CTA ("book through the link", "DM to schedule"); link straight to booking [N14].
- Say the service and the city aloud and as text; local keywords and city hashtags help search [N14].
- On screen: the quoted price and the time taken, at the hand-over and at the end (judgement).
- Name the pro, not only the business: people book a person (judgement, see [N33] on staff spotlights).

## What first-time customers want to see

- The person who will do the job and how they explain it [N33].
- A real result on a real problem [N14].
- What it costs and how long it takes (judgement).
- That the work is careful: clean bench, the right tools, the process shown, not hidden (judgement).
- Parents choosing a tutor or teacher: the teaching style and a child's growing confidence come across
  better on video than in text [N34]; plain words, no jargon [N35].
- How to book or drop in, and the address (judgement).

## What to avoid

- Customer data on screen: phone lock screens, photos, messages, addresses on a parcel or invoice. Film with
  the screen off or the creator's own device (judgement).
- Filming children: scripts use an adult creator as the student; any child on camera needs the parent's
  written consent and should not be identifiable (judgement).
- Clients' homes during cleaning jobs: the owner's consent, no family photos, mail or house numbers (judgement).
- Client reactions only with their permission [N14].
- B2B services with nothing to film (software, consulting): use the meet-the-pro order with the person
  and a screen they control, or skip the venue (judgement, see `../venue_kinds.md`).
- Guarantees ("never breaks again", "grades up in a month"): unsupported claims; describe the work instead
  (judgement, see [N20] on substantiation).
- Gifted or discounted services must be disclosed where the review appears [N21][N22].

## Script ideas

1. Fix in 40 minutes: broken screen close, quoted price, three repair cuts, working phone, time and price.
2. Meet the owner: the pro at the bench, one line on why they started, close on the craft, how to book.
3. First lesson: teacher greets, how a first lesson works, the creator plays a first melody, lesson price.
4. Before and after clean: one room in the same angle, three process cuts, the result, hours and price.
5. Quick tip from the pro: one thing people get wrong, shown with hands, creator repeats it.
6. Photo session behind the scenes: the setup, the photographer directs, one frame shown, package price.
7. What it costs: the pro lists the three most asked services and prices, shot of the shop, booking.
8. Drop-off to pick-up: hand-over at the counter, the wait, the pick-up and the creator's verdict.
